# QR 1.7.0, 12 October 2026

## What changed

- **No size control for the downloads.** The PNG and the SVG always carry 5 cm, or more when a
  code needs it to scan. A document places them at that size instead of filling the page, and
  people resize them wherever they put them, as on other QR sites. "At least … cm, so phones can
  read it" stays under the buttons.
- **The only size choice is the sheet's, and the layouts come first:** 1, 4, 12 or 30 on a page,
  as in 1.6.0.
- **A size of one's own, out of the way:** "Choose your own size" under the layouts opens the
  − / +, never down to a size too small for phones to read, and two choices: "Fill the page" with
  as many as fit, for cutting out, or "Just one". This brings back sheets of 3 cm or 6 cm
  stickers, which 1.6.0's four layouts couldn't print, and one code at a size you pick.
- **Counts carry no sizes any more:**
  - Nobody chooses a file's size now, so a file's `size_cm` (5, or more for a long text) could
    only hint at how long the text is. It's gone.
  - A sheet at a layout sends `codes` (1, 4, 12 or 30).
  - A sheet at a size of one's own sends neither how many nor the size, because both can depend
    on how long the caption or the text is.
  - One bit remains: if a long caption or a dense code greys out the chosen layout, the page
    moves to one with fewer codes, and `codes` says so.

## Promises this release touches

| Promise | How | Ledger |
|---|---|---|
| The page says how small you can print | The "At least … cm" line, under the buttons, without a tap | Tested |
| We count what's done on the page, never what's in it | Row 34 and the README: no sizes are counted; a sheet sends its layout | Tested |
| A feature you use isn't removed without notice | The files' size control goes. Per the rule since robinscode #23, a notice period doesn't hold up a change the maintainer asks for before a product has users; the changelog says it | By hand |

**Taken off the promise list:** the row on the files' print size and the sheet. The maintainer:
how a download comes out isn't a promise, it's how the tool works. The README says it in plain
words, and its tests stay.

**Guards changed:**
- **The files' test** now checks:
  - 5 cm in the PNG and the SVG;
  - that a 1,200-character code's PNG and SVG both carry 9 cm, its smallest;
  - that "At least … cm" shows without a tap.

  It no longer steps a size, because there's no size to step. That dropped the old check that
  the PNG follows a size other than the default. The dense code's 9 cm stands in for it.
- **The sheet test** prints at sizes of one's own and checks how many codes print and their
  width: 6 at 6 cm, 48 at 2 cm. A dense code's own size stops at its smallest, 4 cm, and prints
  12 there.
- **The one-page test** prints sizes of one's own (2 and 6 cm) as well as every layout, with
  each caption, and checks that as many print as the page said.
- **The counts test** checks that a file's count carries no size.
- **The privacy test** also prints at a size of one's own, with what was typed as the caption.

A first version of these tests passed without checking a dense code's own size, a dense code's
PNG, or how many printed; the second review found that, and it's fixed.

## What the maintainer read

Testing 1.6.0, he found the two sizes confusing (one for the downloads behind Change, one for a
sheet's layout), and asked for an opinionated decision: he expected people to want layouts, not a fixed size, and
a single code at a fixed size to be worth supporting but not up front. He saw screenshots of this version, and
said a feature's output isn't a promise, that "never prints a code smaller than it scans" sounded
corporate, when what it means is that we make sure every code prints big enough for phones to
read, and that a change he asks for is his go
to deploy it. Told that a size of one's own filled the page when someone might want one code,
he asked for "Just one" or "Fill the page" there, and for the layouts to stay the main way. Asked
not to just agree, Claude kept "1 on a page" as big as the page allows (one code at a modest size
is what "Just one" is for) and added no more layouts until his testing misses one.

## What agents checked

- **On the maintainer's Raspberry Pi, in Chromium, against the local pages:**
  - the line under the buttons;
  - the layouts, then "Choose your own size" with its steps and how many fit;
  - going back to a layout;
  - the counts each sends;
  - one page on A4 and Letter at 2, 6 and 18 cm with no caption, short, 60-character, unbroken
    and Japanese captions.
- `script.sheetOwn` reuses 1.5.1's sheet line ("12 codes, 5 cm wide, on one page"), already
  translated and checked back.
- **A translation check** back-translated `page.ownSize` ("Choose your own size") in 12 languages:
  - Croatian and French could read as a clothing size or one's height;
  - Turkish was ambiguous;
  - Italian's word order was odd;
  - Portuguese said "another size".

  All five are fixed. "Fill the page" and "Just one" were checked the same way: Portuguese,
  German and Dutch were reworded, and Korean matched to the file's other labels.
- **A second review** (a change to what's counted) found nothing that breaks a promise. It found
  that three tests passed without checking what they were named for, and that a file's size hinted
  at how long the text is. It also found the words below. All are fixed:
  - "the PNG and the SVG always print 5 cm wide" in the changelog: they carry it, and apps that
    ignore a PNG's print size won't print it at 5 cm;
  - this row's missing notice line;
  - a code comment that contradicted the code.

## What nobody checked

- Printing from Firefox or Safari, or with print settings a person changes. The one-page checks
  are Chromium's PDF output only.
- That the printed code's width matches the size the page names: Chromium shrinks content that is
  too wide, and a page count can't see that. It matters more now that a person picks the size.
- A real printer, and a real phone.
- The translations by a native speaker.
